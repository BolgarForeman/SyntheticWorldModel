WORK ORDER 11 — Repair timing and engine fixes (Phase 2)

One pull request, on `wo11/repair-timing`, cut from main after this work order merges.

Why: writing the crew rules as equations found two engine bugs and a spec ambiguity. Ruling them then showed that the world's one repair time is the wrong quantity for a 72-hour window. Hazus gives the time to rebuild a facility, but service comes back much sooner through workarounds. This PR fixes the bugs and gives the world two repair paths. Phase 2 then closes on brute force (Foreman, 2026-10-23: the MIP cross-check is held for after the proposal).

Spec: `docs/design/evidence/Repair_Timing_Ruling_Sheet_v0_1.md`, all five items ruled (Foreman, 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23); `docs/design/evidence/MIP_Crew_Rules_Ruling_Sheet_v0_2.md` Part 3 (E1, E2) and item 11; World_Generator_Structure.md §4.4a and §4.5, "Repair and crews", amended by this PR; CLAUDE.md §2, §7, §9 and §10.

# PART A — THE TWO BUGS

A1 **A cancelled switching trip must not strand a newer one** (`_cancel_switching`, sheet E1). When a de-energised mark clears, cancel only the substation's *unassigned* trip for that episode. Keep the node-to-trip index consistent: a trip that is assigned, finished or cancelled leaves the index, so a newer trip is never orphaned in `jobs`. An assigned trip is still travelled and worked, as now.

A2 **A stock that runs out passes on the remainder** (`_draw`, sheet E2). When a node's first stock covers only part of a step's draw, the next stock is charged the remainder, not the full need again. Stocks drain at the actual draw (spec §4.5).

TESTS: a constructed case for each, which fails on main and passes here: a mark that clears, re-opens and clears again; a node with two stocks on one supply where the first empties mid-step.

# PART B — TWO REPAIR PATHS (ruled R1–R4)

B1 **The rebuild path** is today's Hazus repair: crew work plus the remaining Hazus time (`repair.hazus_restoration_days`, `repair.crew_work_h`). It returns the node to full condition. Ruled item 11: a rebuild job is **not open until its parts-ready hour**. Until then no crew is sent and no crew waits on site. Once open, the job is travel, the 2 h assessment, then the work. The "wait for parts" crew activity is removed.

B2 **Slight and moderate damage have no parts wait** (R2). The job is open at hour 0: the assessment plus crew work (8 h or 24 h) brings the node back to full. The remaining Hazus time stays in the truth manifest as the rebuild figure only.

B3 **Extensive and complete damage come back inside the window only by a quick path** (R3). Add the registry rule `repair.service_restore.quick_paths`, keyed by class. It is grade J, placeholder (open decision: realism ranges), with ruling R3 and decision 0020 as its source:

| Class | Quick path | Crew work (h) | Restored share |
|---|---|---|---|
| distribution_substation | Mobile substation (unchanged: `repair.emergency.mobile_substation`, a pool, no crew) | — | as now |
| mast | Mobile cell site (unchanged: `repair.emergency.mobile_cell_site`, a pool, no crew) | — | as now |
| transmission_substation | Temporary bus or bypass | 24 | 1.0 |
| pumping | Portable pumps | 8 | 1.0 |
| storage | Bypass the tank, pump straight to the network | 4 | 1.0 |
| airport | Rapid runway repair, limited operations | 24 | 0.5 |
| every other class | none: out for the window | — | — |

The restored shares are placeholders (1.0 is full service; 0.5 is the airport's limited operations). They are the design side's judgment and are listed for the rules review. They are not a coder choice.

B4 **A quick-path job is a crew job** (R4). It is open at hour 0 and worked in the default crew order with the same key as the node's rebuild job. It runs travel, the 2 h assessment, then the quick-path work. On completion it raises the node's condition to the restored share, as a mobile unit does. The node's rebuild job stays and, once open (B1), brings it to full. There are no new unit pools. Widespread delay comes from crew and unit contention alone.
- Give the quick-path job its own job-id prefix. Mark its sort position against `repair:` and `switch:` as a `CHOICE:` and report it, since job ids break ties in the crew order.
- A plan (restorative model) lists quick-path jobs like any other job. If a crew's next planned job is not yet open (B1), the crew sits out the steps until it opens. Mark that as a `CHOICE:` too; no pilot plan meets it, because scenario 7 damages lines only.

B5 **Strikes** still name complete damage (ruling Q4). A struck node with a quick path can now come back inside the window. The test in PR 10b that a struck node is out for the whole window becomes: it is out until its quick path finishes, and for the whole window if its class has none.
