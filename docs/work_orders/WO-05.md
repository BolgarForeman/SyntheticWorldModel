PART A — THE MODELING RULES REGISTRY (ruled by the author, 2026-09-29)

Purpose: every rule and number that shapes the world lives in one place. Each one shows where it came from and why, and can be changed later by a reviewed edit, without touching code.

A1 A data file, src/swm/rules/modeling_rules.yaml (package data), holds every rule. Each entry has:
  - id: stable, dotted, for example reserves.hospital.tertiary.generator_fuel_h;
  - title: plain words;
  - value and units, or a small table (for example Hazus days by damage state);
  - applies_to: layer, class and subtype condition;
  - grade: S / L / J, and jurisdiction: TW / JP / US / international;
  - sources: a list, each with citation, URL, the verbatim quote where one exists, and whether it was verified;
  - reasoning: one to three plain sentences on why this value;
  - status: ruled, placeholder, or open, plus the open-decision name if any;
  - ruling: who ruled, the date, and the decision record number;
  - history: earlier values, each with its date and the decision that changed it (empty for now).

A2 What goes in it, in PR 5a:
  - every §4.4a target;
  - the §4.3a dependency table and its cross-table rules (moved out of src/swm/recipe/dependencies.py into the registry, with the same 99 rows and the narrowed supplier lists);
  - the Work Order 4 subtype shares;
  - the attribute ranges;
  - the power sizing margin and power factor;
  - the transmission threshold.
  After this PR, no world-shaping number may live as a bare constant in code. Code reads it from the registry by id. Terrain index mappings may stay in code for now; list them in the report as the next migration.

A3 Loading and validation (src/swm/rules/):
  - The registry is loaded once and validated by a pydantic schema. Every id is unique; every entry has a grade and at least one source, or status placeholder with reasoning; units are present where the value is numeric.
  - The registry's SHA-256 and a registry version string are written into the resolved recipe and stamped on every world, so each world records which rules built it.
  - A recipe may override named rule values for sensitivity runs (for example the tertiary second-feed share). Overrides are listed in the resolved recipe beside the registry hash, never silently.

A4 The Modeling Rules page. `swm rules -o OUT.html` renders the registry as one self-contained HTML page:
  - grouped by stage (dependencies, requirements, reserves, tolerance, failure modes, repair, crews, sizing);
  - each rule shows its value, units, grade, jurisdiction, reasoning, sources with links and quotes, status and ruling;
  - a filter by grade and status, so placeholders and judgment calls are easy to find;
  - a header with the registry version and hash.
  It is for reading and review. It is outside the identity check but deterministic. Post a screenshot in the pull request. The Phase 7 interface will show the same page.

A5 How rules change later: a pull request edits modeling_rules.yaml, moves the old value into history, cites the new source, and adds a decision record. Write this procedure into docs/design/README.md and CLAUDE.md §9 ("rules change only through the registry"). Test that the page and the registry stay in sync, and that an entry without a source or reasoning fails validation.

PART B — REQUIREMENTS (new stage src/swm/stages/requirements.py; spec §4.4, §4.4a)

B1 Stage order becomes layers → requirements → couplings. Requirements depend only on node class, subtype and size attributes. Draw them from new streams requirements/<layer>.

B2 For each node and each commodity it consumes under §4.3a, draw its requirement from the §4.4a target:
  - electricity in MW;
  - water in m³/day;
  - fuel in GJ/day for thermal generation, and the backup-generator burn only as a rate for later use;
  - telecom as binary.
  Use the size attribute the node already has. The port and airport size-to-throughput tables go in the registry as placeholders anchored to Taiwan's published throughput.

B3 The draw method (spec §4.4): one skewed quantile table, lognormal-shaped, built offline and stored in the registry as decimal constants with provenance. It is scaled to median 1 and clipped at the 0.5% and 99.5% quantiles. Heterogeneity sets the spread as a standard deviation relative to the target; the compliance gap shifts the median. Runtime arithmetic is +, −, ×, ÷ and sqrt only. Put the offline table builder in tools/ (not in src/). Test the realised spread and median against the levers, within a stated tolerance.

B4 Peak load = requirement × the peak factor (registry, placeholder 1.3).

PART D — RESERVES AND BACKUP (src/swm/stages/buffers.py)

D1 Stock reserves per node and dependency, from §4.4a, drawn with the Part B method:
  - generator fuel at essential load;
  - batteries;
  - hospital water at the emergency ration;
  - supplies;
  - blood stock;
  - distribution storage;
  - LNG, oil and coal stocks (oil pro rata by tank capacity);
  - pre-positioned pump-station generators at a recipe share.
  Battery and generator hours add. Fire-reserve water is excluded.

D2 Presence of backup comes from the subtype attributes (standby_fuel, has a generator, has a battery), not from the compliance gap. The gap is capped at 95 in the schema.

D3 Generator availability: a separate per-generator draw of fail-to-start and fail-to-run from the registry (Marqusee & Jenket 2020).

D4 Fuel resupply parameters on buffer_resupply edges: 12 h lead time and 24 h of fuel per delivery, from the registry. They are recorded here and used by the dynamics.

PART E — COUPLING STRENGTH AND THE UNDISTURBED-SUPPLY CHECK

E1 κ = min(1, available supply ÷ requirement), where available supply is the supplier's capacity shared pro rata among its consumers. Record the uncapped ratio as headroom. A telecom edge's κ is its public share.

E2 World check (spec §4.4): in the undisturbed world every consumer has R_φ ≥ 1 for every commodity. On failure, report which nodes and commodities fall short, and stop. Never regenerate silently. Add it to `swm check`.

PART F — TOLERANCE AND FAILURE MODE

F1 Tolerance buffers for telecom per §4.4a: T hours and level d per class. The hosted-records hospital ramps linearly from 0.5 h to 4 h. d_eff = 1 − κ(1 − d). For substations, telecom loss is recorded as a slower-switching-and-repair flag, not a supply cut.

F2 Failure mode per node and commodity:
  - defaults: physical flow trip; logistic, control and service throttle;
  - class departures per §4.4a;
  - hospitals step to the essential fraction on generator power;
  - heterogeneity 10% is the share departing from the class default;
  - the combining rule is the minimum across shortfalls, not their product.
  Record it; the dynamics (Work Order 7) applies it.

PART G — REPAIR (src/swm/stages/repair.py)

G1 Per node class and damage state (slight, moderate, extensive, complete), from the registry:
  - Hazus MR4 restoration means and standard deviations;
  - crew work hours (8 h slight, 24 h moderate, placeholders);
  - lead time = Hazus mean − crew work, floored at 0.
  Reservoirs are unrepairable in the window. Draw per node with the Part B method.

G2 Emergency repair options per §4.4a:
  - distribution substation: mobile substation, 12–24 h including travel, about 25 MVA against the node's normal peak load;
  - mast: mobile cell site, about 14 h, capacity 0.5.
  No other class has one. Record both modes per eligible node; the solver chooses.

G3 Travel time over the road network from the crew base is its own term, recorded per node.

PART H — CREWS AND CREW BASES

H1 Rename the recipe's depot count to crew_base. It is distinct from the fuel depot class; migrate the schema and record it.

H2 Site crew bases in towns by road access. The pilot has:
