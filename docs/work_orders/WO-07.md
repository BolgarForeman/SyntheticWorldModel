# PR 7b — The engine

PART D — THE ENGINE (src/swm/dynamics/)

D1 A pure engine: (world, scenario, actions, crew policy, horizon, relaxed limits) → trajectories.
- **Actions** in this PR are only "protect" (the element starts undamaged, the fortification convention) and "add reserve". No solver is built.
- The generator never imports it, and it never imports error induction or emission. Extend the separation test.

D2 **State and supply** (spec §4.5):
- state = condition × supply level;
- supply level = the minimum response across commodities;
- edge delivery = κ × g_δ(supplier state), with g_δ linear for physical flow and service, and the threshold θ for logistic and control;
- pro rata sharing when a supplier is short;
- responses: throttle, trip at 0.9, or the floor;
- the telecom tolerance acts first where a class has a row;
- a failed line passes nothing.

D3 **Stocks and refill:**
- drain at the actual draw;
- generator fail-to-start and fail-to-run from the drawn values;
- deliveries at 12 h after power loss, then every 24 h, while the depot is up and a road reaches it;
- refill:
  - batteries refill over 12 h;
  - water storage refills from the pumps' spare capacity;
  - purified and make-up water refill from the main;
  - tanks refill only by delivery;
  - national stocks never refill.

D4 **Repair and crews:**
- a crew's sequence: assigned → travel → crew-bound lead time → repair once its parts are in → travel to the next job;
- the parts clock starts at the end of the storm (hour 0 in Phase 1);
- crews move job to job and never return to base;
- the default crew order, with ties going to the shortest travel, then the lowest id;
- emergency units go to eligible damaged nodes in the same order;
- lines: one job per line, with travel to each failure point in turn (road, then off road), 6 h per failure point, or 72 h for a collapsed tower.

The order of failure points within a line is your CHOICE; state it.

D5 **Manual switching.** When a repair should re-energize a substation whose telecom is down:
- power returns only after a crew travels there and spends `repair.manual_switching_h` on site;
- the trip is a job in the crew queue at the same priority as the repair that triggered it;
- it does not apply when the repair is at that substation.

D6 **Time:**
- the storm acts at hour 0;
- 15-minute steps;
- within each step, recompute until nothing changes, up to 100 passes;
- a step that hits the cap is recorded in the run, and the run reports it;
- the horizon comes from the call (default 72 h).

D7 **Arithmetic:**
- only + − × ÷ and sqrt;
- states compared exactly;
- node update order fixed by id;
- no set or dict-order dependence.

D8 **Relaxed limits.** A relaxed stock never runs out, and a relaxed delay is zero. Unlimited crews means every job gets its own crew at once. Removed dependencies are gone from the world before the run starts.

PART E — RUNS AND OUTPUT

E1 `swm simulate --scenario N [--horizon H] [--relax id ...]`:
- prints a trajectory table: every hour, each end-use node's state, and the stocks that are draining;
- plots a timeline: end-use nodes on one axis, hours on the other, with crew jobs as bars, written to the preview folder;
- `--node ID` prints one node in full.

E2 **Reference runs:**
- the pilot's 8 scenarios, 72 h, no actions, the default crew order;
- saved as golden files stamped with the horizon, the policy and the registry hash;
- per run: every node's state at every step; every stock level; every crew's position and job; every repair completion; and unweighted hours out per end-use node (a fraction when supply is partial).

E3 **Summary table in the report.** Per scenario:
- unweighted hours out, summed over end-use nodes;
- the end-use node out longest;
- when the last repair completes;
- the most passes any step needed.

PART F — CHECKS AND TESTS

F1 **Calm-world check:**
- with no damage, every node holds state 1, every stock stays full, and no crew, delivery or tolerance clock moves for the whole horizon;
- runs on every world in `swm check`, and fails the build on any deviation.

F2 **Degradation profiles.** One test per profile in Degradation_Profiles_v0_2.md:
- isolate the node, cut one supply at hour 0, run 72 h, and compare against the profile;
- each limit is also run at its relaxed value.

F3 **Monotonicity:**
- relaxing a limit never raises any node's hours out (property test over random single relaxations on the pilot);
- protecting an element never raises hours out.

F4 **Removal versus relaxing:** on a constructed case, an unlimited stock and a removed dependency give the same supply level, but the run records them differently.

F5 **Crew logic:**
- on a constructed case: the crew-bound lead time repeats per job; parts run in parallel; crews take jobs in order;
- a manual switching trip is created only when telecom is down and the repair is elsewhere.

F6 **Convergence:** a constructed loop that cannot settle hits the cap and is reported, not hidden.

F7 **Identity:** the reference runs are byte-identical on Windows and Linux. Explain every golden change.
