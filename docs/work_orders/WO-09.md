# PR 9a — The hand-trace pack

The pack's job: a person with only the printout, the spec and a calculator can play the scenario forward and get the engine's answer. Nothing in the world changes; only the printout.

A1 Split the pack.
- `trace_pack/` holds the blind pack: map and tables, no results.
- `trace_pack/answer/` holds the reference-run timeline (PDF pages and `timeline.csv`), printed separately so a tracer can set it aside.

A2 Add tables (each as PDF pages and CSV):
- **Links:** every within-layer link with id, layer, from, to, length (km), on towers or not, and the commodity it carries; and one line stating how a node with more than one in-feed combines them, as the engine does it.
- **Damage:** for each failed line, each failure point's position (km along the line), its travel time from the crew base (road km, off-road km, hours) and its crew work; for each damaged facility, damage state, crew work and parts lead time.
- **Crews:** crew count, base and its location; travel time from the base to every node; each repair job's priority group in the default crew order, with the Lifeline weights used; the rule the engine uses for travel between jobs, in one sentence.
- **Node additions:** end-use node (yes/no) and its service weight; capacities (plant MW, treatment and pump m³/day); each stock's quantity, units and draw per hour; generator fail-to-start and fail-to-run draws; the tolerance row (T, d, κ, d_eff) and whether manual switching applies.
- **Dependency additions:** feed configuration (single or dual) and whether the edge is a fuel-resupply edge.

A3 Add a one-page rules sheet: every number the engine uses in a trace, with its registry id — step length, settling cap, trip point, floor, threshold, crew-bound lead, line work per failure point and per towered failure point, manual switching time, off-road speed, battery recharge, delivery lead time and interval, refill rules, horizon. Read from the registry, never typed.

A4 The map:
- frame on the island, not the storm track; show the track as an arrow or inset with its closest approach;
- an inset at a larger scale wherever labels collide (the eastern cluster);
- label every line with its id; mark every failure point;
- keep it one page, byte-identical on both platforms.

A5 Tests:
- every value in the new tables equals its source in the world or registry (no hand-typed numbers);
- the blind pack contains no reference-run value;
- the pack is byte-identical on both platforms (goldens updated, every change explained).

Report: the new map as an image, and the list of tables.

# PR 9g — The trace game (Part H)

Why: PR 9a's pack holds every number but cannot be worked by a person: dense tables, tiny type, no way in. Foreman ruled (2026-10-11) that the pack becomes a paper teaching game. It teaches the mechanics, never the solution, and his son's play-test of it is the Phase 1 hand-trace acceptance test. Ruling sheet: `docs/design/evidence/Trace_Pack_Ruling_Sheet_v0_2.md` (P1–P11). The ruling sheet governs.

Standing rules for every piece:
- every number and rule is generated from the world and the registry, never typed;
- nothing in the teaching text, rule cards or drills names or describes any node, line or event of the mission scenario;
- the engine's answer appears only in `trace_pack/answer/`.

PART H — THE TRACE GAME

H1 Command and output. `swm trace-pack --scenario N [--board-size S]` writes `trace_pack/`:
- `game/` — everything a player gets;
- `answer/` — sealed;
- `data/` — PR 9a's tables and CSVs, moved here unchanged.

`--board-size` is one of `11x17` (default), `tiled-letter` (letter sheets with trim and join marks), `24x36`, `36x48`. All sizes come from one layout. Everything is byte-identical on both platforms (P1, P9).

H2 Node cards (P3).
- One card per node, 9 to a letter sheet with cut lines, nothing under 9 pt.
- Each card holds:
  - **header:** id, class, end use and weight;
  - **Needs:** for each supply: supplier, κ, transfer form, response, and for own-layer supply the response the engine uses;
  - **Back-up:** stocks in hours with refill; telecom fallback T, then d_eff; manual switching;
  - **Supplies:** the nodes it supplies, switched-on only.
- Symbols: ⚡ trip, ◐ throttle, ▁ floor, ⏳ fallback, ▮ stock, with a legend card.
- Cards carry no damage and no scenario content; the same deck serves every scenario of a world.

H3 Rule cards with drills (P4).
- One card per mechanic, in the order of the ruling sheet:
  - weakest supply;
  - throttle, trip and floor;
  - how a partial supply passes (linear or threshold);
  - two in-feeds into one node;
  - telecom fallback;
  - stocks drain and refill;
  - fuel deliveries;
  - crew sequence;
  - crew priority order and ties;
  - travel between jobs;
  - manual switching;
  - time and rounding to steps.
- Front: the rule in one or two sentences, the registry value(s) inserted at build with their ids, and an illustration on generic nodes A, B, C.
- Back: one drill and its answer.
- Wording comes from text files in `docs/design/trace_game/rule_cards/`, one per card, with registry placeholders. The design side supplies them; until then use clearly marked DRAFT text written from the spec.
- **Each drill is verified:** a test builds the drill's tiny constructed world, runs the engine, and checks the printed answer. A drill whose answer the engine does not reproduce fails the build.

H4 The board (P5).
- Network side: every node in its layer column and every switched-on link and dependency, arrows coloured by commodity. Nodes are ordered within columns to reduce crossings, deterministically; your method is a CHOICE.
- Map side: island, crew base, every node, roads, with travel hours from the base printed at each node and at each line's failure points.
- Hour track 0–72 along the edge.
- Nothing is highlighted; the damage is placed by the player.

H5 Mission briefing (P6). One page from the scenario:
- the storm's track and closest approach;
- every failed line with its failure points (km along the line, travel hours from the base, crew work);
- every damaged facility with damage state;
- crews and base;
- horizon.

It states what failed, never what it causes.

H6 Sheets (P6, P10): crew sheet, event log, outage sheet (every end-use node pre-listed with its weight), decision log, and the play-test feedback sheet (time, question or stall, card or page).

H7 Token sheet: damage markers, one pawn per crew, out markers, a clock token, stock clocks.

H8 Sealed answer (P6, P7), in `answer/`:
- the engine's event timeline, readable: one line per event, exact time and the step it takes effect;
- each end-use node's outage intervals and hours out;
- the score: weighted service-hours lost (Lifeline weights) as the headline, unweighted hours out beside it.

H9 Field manual and debrief (P8, P7). Typeset from `docs/design/trace_game/field_manual.md` and `debrief.md`. The design side supplies both after Foreman rules them; until then, a placeholder page marked DRAFT.

H10 Tests:
- every number on every card and sheet equals its source;
- no file under `game/` contains a reference-run value (changing the run leaves `game/` byte-identical);
- no teaching text names a node or line id of the world;
- each drill is verified by the engine (H3);
- the score in `answer/` equals the reference run's;
- identity on both platforms at every board size;
- goldens updated, every change explained.

Report:
- images of one node-card sheet, one rule-card sheet, the board at 11×17, the briefing and the outage sheet;
- your crossing-reduction CHOICE;
- the full list of files.
