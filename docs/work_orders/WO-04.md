PART A — THE DEPENDENCY TABLE IN THE SCHEMA

A1 Put §4.3a's table in the schema as one immutable table: one row per (supplier layer, consumer class, subtype condition). Each row holds:
  - dependency (core / optional / none);
  - dependency type;
  - role (primary_supply / buffer_resupply);
  - commodity;
  - eligible supplier classes, with any product restriction (for example import_terminal with product lng);
  - the evidence grade (S / L / J), and a US marker where given;
  - a one-line source note naming the evidence key.
  The whole table is CHOICE, placeholder (open decision: realism ranges).

A2 Subtype conditions are predicates on the consumer's subtype attributes (Part B), for example "generation where fuel_kind = coal". Every (coupling, consumer class) pair from §4.3a resolves to exactly one row for any combination of attribute values. Test that.

A3 Recipe settings added to the couplings section:
  - two_way becomes two_way_pct, 0–100 (the share of allowed mutual node pairs that are built). Default 50, pilot 0. The pilot keeps the hand trace simple; say so in the fixture.
  - public_telecom_share_pct per consumer layer, for control edges under §4.3a rule 1. Defaults: generation and transmission 20, distribution 50, water 50, pipelines 50.
  - tertiary_second_feed_pct, default 50 (rule 5).
  - transmission_connection_threshold_mw (rule 4). Give it a placeholder value and a reason.
  All are CHOICE placeholders with units in their names. Schema v1 stays open until v0.1.0, so no version bump.

PART C — POWER SIZING (spec §4.2)

- Distribution substations keep their footprint-based size.
- A transmission substation's capacity is at least the total of the distribution substations it feeds in the power links, times the margin.
- Total generation capacity is total distribution capacity times the reserve margin, converted with the power factor, and split across plants by draw.
- Use one reserve margin (1.3) and one power factor (0.9), both CHOICE placeholders.
- Test the ratios.
- Report the pilot's new figures. The old figure was 1,207 MW against 121 MVA of distribution.

PART E — THE COUPLINGS STAGE (src/swm/stages/couplings.py)

E1 Terms. A dependency is one consumer node's need for one supplier layer's commodity. A coupling edge is one supplier node serving that need.

E2 Scope.
  - Pairs involving an off layer produce nothing.
  - Hidden layers couple like emitted ones.
  - Switched-off pairs under generate_and_hide are built in full, with switched_on = false. Under not_generated they are absent.
  - Each pair draws from its own child streams, couplings/<purpose>/<supplier>-<consumer>. Switching one pair on or off must never move another pair's edges. Register the new paths; no existing stream may move.

E3 Which dependencies exist. Resolve each consumer's row from §4.3a by its class and subtype attributes.
  - Core rows always exist.
  - Optional rows are added by density: round(density/100 × optional count) across the world, by largest remainder, chosen by draw.

E4 Feed configuration, for primary-supply dependencies other than transport.
  - Apply feed_mix with exact largest-remainder counts.
  - Two full: two distinct supplier nodes, either covers the need. Two half: two distinct supplier nodes, both needed.
  - A dependency with only one eligible supplier node stays single, with a note.
  - The tertiary second feed (rule 5) is drawn separately and recorded as two full feeds.
  - Report the realised shares.

E5 Transport dependencies (rule 3). List every eligible origin that reaches the consumer over the transport links (the road network from Work Order 3), with feed configuration any_reachable. If no origin reaches it, record the dependency with no supplier, plus a note. Never silently drop it.

E6 Buffer resupply (rule 2). Fuel edges with role buffer_resupply are built like any other edge, carry their role, and take their suppliers by the usual choice. Their conditional effect belongs to the dynamics, not here. Record, for each one, the id of the consumer's power dependency it backs.

E7 Supplier choice (non-transport edges).
  - Each switched-on pair has one hub, drawn among its eligible supplier nodes. The draw is weighted by capacity where the class has one, and uniform otherwise.
  - Concentration sets the share of first feeds assigned to the hub: round(concentration/100 × count), by draw. At 0 there is no hub pull, and first feeds go to the nearest eligible supplier.
  - Supplier-not-nearest then plants among the remaining dependencies with at least two eligible suppliers. It gives each a non-nearest supplier, weighted by 1 ÷ distance, until the pair's realised non-nearest share reaches the recipe's.
    - Hub assignments that are not nearest count toward the share.
    - Concentration wins in a conflict; notes record any excess or shortfall.
  - A second feed goes to the nearest eligible supplier other than the first.
  - "Nearest" means straight-line distance between node points, with ties broken by node id.

E8 Public telecom share (rule 1). Each control edge on a power, water or pipeline consumer carries public_share_pct from the recipe setting for its consumer layer. Record it; the dynamics uses it.

E9 Two-way (spec §4.3). Find every node pair where each could depend on the other under the table and the switched-on pairs. Build round(two_way_pct/100 × count) of them as mutual pairs, by draw, overriding supplier choice where needed. At 0, forbid mutual pairs: where one would arise, the later edge in canonical order takes the next eligible supplier, or the dependency gets a note. Report the counts.

E10 Geometry. Each edge is a straight line from supplier to consumer, with length_km and crosses_sea. It is a dependency, not a route.

E11 Output: a frozen dataclass holding:
  - edges: id (SWM-CPL-0001), supplier, consumer, layers, commodity, dependency type, role, transfer form, feed configuration, feed group, dependency id, backs_dependency (for buffer resupply), public_share_pct, switched_on, is_nearest, is_hub, mutual partner, length_km, crosses_sea;
  - dependencies: id, consumer, supplier layer, table row key, core or optional, feed configuration, edge ids, notes;
  - statistics:
    - per pair: dependencies, edges, feed shares, non-nearest share, hub share, mutual pairs, notes;
    - overall: coupling edges, intra-layer links, and the total.

No file writing inside the stage.

PART G — TESTS
  - Identity (golden): the pilot's edge and dependency tables and statistics reproduce byte for byte on both platforms.
  - Table fidelity: every edge's supplier class and product are eligible for its row; every core row exists for every switched-on pair; no none row produces an edge.
  - Density: 0 gives core only; 100 gives every allowed dependency; the count rises monotonically in between.
  - Feed mix: realised counts equal the largest-remainder counts; two-feed dependencies have two distinct suppliers; the property holds for random shares totalling 100.
  - Concentration and not-nearest:
    - at 100 the hub carries every dependency it is eligible for;
    - at 0 with not-nearest 0, every first feed is the nearest;
    - otherwise the share matches, or a note is present.
  - Two-way: 0 gives no mutual pair; 100 gives every allowed pair; the count rises monotonically in between.
  - Transport: every listed origin really reaches the consumer over the road links; an unreachable consumer carries a note.
  - Buffer resupply: every buffer_resupply edge points to an existing power dependency of the same consumer.
  - States: an off layer has no edges; a hidden layer couples in full; generate_and_hide differs from switched on only in the flag.
  - Streams:
    - turning one pair off (not_generated) leaves every other pair's edges byte-identical;
    - terrain and layers are unchanged by any couplings setting;
    - only couplings/* streams are drawn.
  - Separation: emission code cannot import the couplings output. Extend the CLAUDE.md §3 test to the new fields.
  - Pilot: the total edge count (intra-layer links plus switched-on coupling edges) is reported. If it falls outside 40–50, report it in the pull request; do not tune.
